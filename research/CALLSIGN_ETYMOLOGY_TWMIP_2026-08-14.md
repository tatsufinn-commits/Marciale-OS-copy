# 2. WHY *IMYT-PR* IS THE PRECISE WORD — AND NOT SIMPLY "WILL" `[VERIFIED — external]`

Egyptological scholarship confirms the term and, critically, **sharpens the Commander's point beyond what a generic "will" would carry:**

* *Imyt-pr* documents are **the earliest contracts on record** in ancient Egypt, literally *"that which is in the house."* Frequently glossed as "wills," but a better rendering is **"(property) transfer document."**
* **⚖️ THE DECISIVE DETAIL:** they were used to transfer property **to someone *other than* the person who would inherit if the owner died intestate.** An *imyt-pr* exists **precisely when the default line of succession is not the one being followed.**
* They were **sealed and filed in a central government office** — a public, recorded instrument, not a private wish.
* Beneficiaries were *"almost always relatives"* — the ordinary heirs. **An outsider beneficiary is the rare, exceptional case.**
* **Offices, not only land, passed by *imyt-pr*.** In earlier periods an office **could not be sold — only transferred by *imyt-pr***. `[P. UC 32037: a son inherits his father's office; an earlier imyt-pr is cancelled and replaced]`
* An *imyt-pr* could **cancel and replace a prior one** — the instrument is revisable by the living authority.

*Sources: Fathom Archive / University of Chicago (Women's Legal Rights in Ancient Egypt; Ancient Egyptian Society and Family Life); Sandra Lippert, Universität Tübingen, UCLA Encyclopedia of Egyptology (2013).*
